Quick Tip: What you eat before, during, and after training directly impacts how fast you improve. Treat nutrition as part of your training plan, not separate from it.

Fueling Your Performance

🍽️ Pre-Workout Nutrition

  • Eat a balanced meal 2–3 hours before training (carbs + protein + low fat).
  • If you're short on time, a lighter snack 30–60 minutes before works (banana, rice cake with PB, etc.).
  • Stay hydrated before sessions — start sipping water well before you train.
  • Avoid high-fat or high-fiber foods right before intense sessions as they slow digestion.

⚡ Post-Workout Recovery Nutrition

  • Eat within 30–60 minutes of finishing to begin muscle repair.
  • Target 20–40g of quality protein (chicken, eggs, Greek yogurt, protein shake).
  • Pair with fast-digesting carbohydrates to replenish glycogen stores.
  • Don't skip this window — it's one of the most important meals of the day for athletes.

💧 Hydration

  • Drink 16–24 oz of water 1–2 hours before practice or games.
  • Replace fluids during activity — don't wait until you're thirsty.
  • Monitor urine color: pale yellow means properly hydrated; dark means drink more.
  • In hot or high-intensity conditions, add electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium).
  • Avoid sugary drinks and energy drinks during activity.

🏋️ Building & Maintaining Muscle

  • Aim for 0.7–1g of protein per pound of bodyweight daily.
  • Distribute protein across 4–5 meals rather than loading it all at once.
  • Prioritize whole food sources: lean meats, fish, legumes, dairy, eggs.
  • Creatine monohydrate is one of the most researched and effective supplements for power athletes.

🥗 Daily Nutrition Habits

  • Eat consistent meals throughout the day — skipping meals hurts energy and recovery.
  • Build each plate around lean protein, complex carbs, and vegetables.
  • Healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil) support hormone function and joint health.
  • Limit processed foods, excess sugar, and alcohol — all slow recovery and adaptation.

📋 In-Season vs. Off-Season

  • In-season: prioritize calorie maintenance, recovery fueling, and staying consistent.
  • Off-season: this is your window to add lean mass — slightly increase calories and training load.
  • Pre-camp: build up energy stores in the weeks before camp starts.
  • Track what you eat — awareness is the first step to better habits.
Quick Tip: Training breaks your body down. Recovery is where improvement happens. Prioritize sleep, movement quality, and stress management as seriously as your workouts.

Recovery Methods

😴 Sleep

  • Sleep is the most powerful recovery tool available — aim for 8–10 hours per night.
  • Prioritize consistent sleep and wake times, even on weekends.
  • Reduce screen time 30–60 minutes before bed to improve sleep quality.
  • Keep your sleep environment cool, dark, and quiet.
  • Naps (20–30 min) can supplement overnight sleep, especially on double-session days.

🧊 Cold Therapy

  • Ice baths or cold water immersion (10–15 min at 50–59°F) can reduce muscle soreness.
  • Cold therapy is most effective after high-volume or contact-heavy sessions.
  • Contrast therapy (alternating cold and warm) can improve circulation and recovery speed.
  • Cryotherapy chambers are an option at some facilities — short exposure (2–3 min) at extreme cold.

🧘 Soft Tissue Work

  • Foam rolling (5–10 min post-training) helps reduce tightness and improve range of motion.
  • Massage therapy — even once a month — supports long-term tissue health.
  • Lacrosse balls are great for targeting specific tight spots (feet, glutes, shoulders).
  • Percussion devices (Theragun, Hyperice) can speed up pre and post-session warm-up and cooldown.

🚶 Active Recovery

  • Light movement on rest days (walking, swimming, cycling) keeps blood flowing without taxing the body.
  • Dynamic stretching before training prepares joints and muscles for load.
  • Static stretching after training (hold 30–60 sec) helps restore flexibility.
  • Yoga or mobility sessions 1–2x/week can significantly reduce injury risk over a season.

🛁 Heat Therapy

  • Sauna sessions (15–20 min) can support cardiovascular conditioning and relaxation.
  • Heat helps loosen tight muscles before lower-intensity work or mobility sessions.
  • Don't use heat immediately after high-intensity sessions — allow core temp to normalize first.
  • Contrast with cold exposure for amplified circulation and recovery benefits.

🧠 Mental Recovery

  • Mental fatigue is as real as physical fatigue — schedule time to genuinely unplug.
  • Breathing exercises (box breathing, diaphragmatic breathing) lower cortisol and help with sleep.
  • Visualization and mindfulness practices can sharpen focus and reduce performance anxiety.
  • Talk to coaches, teammates, or counselors if mental load feels unmanageable — it's a strength, not a weakness.

Recovery Scheduling

📅 During the Season

  • Day after game: prioritize sleep, light walking, and nutrition replenishment.
  • Mid-week: controlled soft tissue work and light mobility to keep the body ready.
  • Day before game: avoid anything that leaves you sore — stick to activation and mental prep.

🌱 Off-Season Recovery

  • Take 2–4 weeks of true rest after the season before starting a new training cycle.
  • Use this window to address nagging injuries, improve mobility, and recharge mentally.
  • Gradually reintroduce volume — don't jump back to full intensity immediately.
Quick Tip: Great players know exactly what their position demands. Train your role first, then build the traits that separate you from everyone else at that spot.

Position Guides

🎯 Quarterback (QB)

  • Own pre-snap recognition: identify shell coverage, leverage, and pressure indicators before the snap.
  • Improve footwork timing so drops match route concepts (3-step, 5-step, and movement throws).
  • Build throw consistency under pressure: eyes downfield, balanced base, quick reset mechanics.
  • Target faster processing with film study: coverage ID, progression speed, and checkdown discipline.

💥 Running Back (RB)

  • Develop vision and patience to press the aiming point, then explode through the correct gap.
  • Train acceleration and deceleration for one-cut efficiency and better change of direction.
  • Improve pass protection fundamentals: stance, strike timing, and blitz pickup communication.
  • Become a complete back by improving route running and reliable hands out of the backfield.

👐 Wide Receiver (WR)

  • Master release packages versus press: foot-fire, split release, and hand-fighting at the line.
  • Sharpen route detail: stem discipline, leverage reading, and violent breaks at the top.
  • Improve ball tracking and late hands to win contested catches without tipping defenders.
  • Target blocking effort and perimeter toughness to create big plays after the catch.

🧱 Tight End (TE)

  • Train as a dual-threat: in-line blocking technique plus receiver-level route precision.
  • Develop hand placement and pad level to anchor against edge defenders in run and pass sets.
  • Improve body control in traffic for catches over the middle and red-zone finishes.
  • Target versatile alignment value: attached, slot, wing, and backfield assignments.

🛡️ Offensive Line (OL)

  • Win with stance, first step, and pad level before hand combat even begins.
  • Build pass-pro footwork: set points, independent hands, and interior power absorption.
  • Improve run-block finish with leverage, hip roll, and sustained contact control.
  • Target communication on calls, twists, stunts, and front adjustments every rep.

🧱 Tackle (OT)

  • Own edge pass protection: kick-slide depth, width control, and hand placement against speed rushers.
  • Improve anchor strength versus bull rush while keeping chest clean.
  • Develop backside and frontside run-block footwork for zone and gap schemes.
  • Target recovery ability after initial contact to prevent late pressure.

🧱 Guard (OG)

  • Win in tight spaces with pad level, hip power, and violent hand placement.
  • Improve pull and trap timing to hit landmarks at full speed under control.
  • Develop interior pass-pro reactions to twists, stunts, and delayed pressure.
  • Target finish mentality without losing balance or leverage.

🧱 Center (C)

  • Make accurate, fast snaps from multiple stances without losing blocking leverage.
  • Own protection calls and front identification to keep the entire line synced.
  • Improve lateral quickness for reach blocks and second-level fits.
  • Target communication clarity pre-snap on every play.

🧨 Defensive Line (DL)

  • Explode off the ball with consistent get-off timing and low pad entry.
  • Develop a pass-rush plan: primary move, counter, and finish angle to the QB.
  • Improve block destruction with hands, eyes, and hip leverage to control gaps.
  • Target pursuit conditioning so effort and power stays high late in drives.

🗡️ EDGE (EDGE)

  • Build elite get-off and bend mechanics to threaten the corner every snap.
  • Develop speed-to-power conversion so tackles cannot sit on one move.
  • Improve run-setting discipline: outside arm free, edge integrity, and squeeze technique.
  • Target a planned rush menu with counters for each protection look.

🧭 Linebacker (LB)

  • Train read steps and key progression (guard flow, back action, and route concepts).
  • Improve tackling consistency: leverage entry, near-foot contact, and wrap-through finish.
  • Develop zone drops and match rules so coverage value matches run-defense impact.
  • Target communication leadership as the front-seven signal caller.

⚡ Cornerback (CB)

  • Master press and off-man technique with disciplined eyes and patient footwork.
  • Improve transition speed (backpedal to drive) and recovery ability on vertical routes.
  • Build ball production with late-play timing, high-point skills, and hand-eye work.
  • Target route study by split, formation, and down-and-distance tendencies.

🔒 Safety (S)

  • Own deep-field angles and communication to eliminate explosive plays.
  • Improve open-field tackling and leverage in alley-fit situations.
  • Develop disguise and post-snap range to affect the QB's first read.
  • Target faster route recognition through formation and motion study.

🦵 Kicker (K)

  • Train repeatable approach steps and plant-foot consistency for ball striking accuracy.
  • Improve leg speed and contact quality for field goal distance and kickoff depth.
  • Develop pressure routines: same breath, same tempo, same setup every rep.
  • Target directional kickoff control to support coverage strategy.
