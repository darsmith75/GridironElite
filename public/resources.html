What Coaches Want to See

  • Plays that translate to their level: speed, physicality, technique, and football IQ.
  • Consistent effort, not just one splash play.
  • Clear evidence of your role responsibilities within the scheme.
  • Situational value: third down, red zone, special teams, and late-game reps.

Film Length

  • Aim for a concise highlight: typically 3 to 5 minutes.
  • If you have more strong clips, create position-focused versions rather than one long video.
  • Keep full-game film available separately for deeper evaluation.
  • Update throughout the season instead of waiting for year-end.

Best Plays First

  • Your first 20 to 30 seconds should show your highest-level traits immediately.
  • Open with 4 to 6 clips that define you as a player.
  • Include variety early: different alignments, situations, and competition levels.
  • Do not bury your best reps at the end.

Common Mistakes

  • Too many low-impact clips and not enough true evaluation reps.
  • No pre-snap indicator or spotlight for where you line up.
  • Overuse of dramatic effects, long intros, or heavy music distractions.
  • Outdated film that does not represent your current size, speed, or role.

What college coaches want to see on your film

EDGE

Your highlight film is your resume. Show coaches you win with skill, speed, effort, and football IQ.

EDGE Highlight Film Blueprint

Core Evaluation Areas

01Effort: Relentless motor on every play.
02Technique: Use your hands and win with skill.
03Speed: Get off the ball and close fast.
04Play recognition: Read, react, and make plays.
05Production: Make game-changing plays.

Highlights: What to Show

  1. Get-off and first step: Beat the tackle's punch and create an immediate advantage.
  2. Pass-rush moves: Show variety—speed, power, and finesse—and finish the rush.
  3. Finishing plays: Include sacks, QB hits, hurries, strip-sacks, and forced fumbles.
  4. Run defense: Set the edge, pursue, contain, and stop the run.
  5. Impact plays: Show turnovers, third-down stops, and goal-line stands.
  6. Consistency and motor: Include effort and hustle on every down, not only splash plays.

What Coaches Evaluate

  • Alignment, stance, pad level, and versatility along the line.
  • First-step quickness and explosion off the ball.
  • Hand usage: stab, swipe, rip, and swim; disengage and keep blockers guessing.
  • Speed-to-power, pocket collapse, and ability to drive through the play.
  • Rush angles, bend, and the discipline not to run past the play.
  • Formation recognition, run-versus-pass reaction, and contain responsibility.
  • Production: sacks, QB hits, hurries, tackles for loss, and forced fumbles.

Film Tips

  • Lead with your best plays and prioritize quality over quantity.
  • Show range: pass rush, run defense, and pursuit.
  • End with your best play; keep effort and versatility visible throughout.

What Separates You

  • Relentless effort and a high motor on every rep.
  • Versatility across the line.
  • Leadership on and off the field.
  • Film that shows how hard you work.
🏈
Final Tip

Coaches watch thousands of players. Make your film memorable: it is your resume, and every rep should show why you deserve a roster spot.

What to highlight on film

TE

Show college coaches you can win your role and make plays. Your film should show that you win consistently and make plays that help your team score and win.

Tight End Highlight Film Blueprint

Every TE Film Should Show

01Effort: Play with energy every snap.
02Technique: Use great technique consistently.
03Versatility: Line up in multiple spots and do multiple jobs.
04Football IQ: Make smart reads and be in the right place.
05Toughness: Block, compete, and win in traffic.
06Reliability: Be a target your team can count on.
07Impact: Make plays that change the game.

Highlights by Tight End Role

Y · In-Line Tight End

The in-line blocker and play-action threat. Alignment: attached to the line.

Key roles: in-line blocker (run and pass), play-action threat, short-to-intermediate receiver, and red-zone target.

1. In-Line Blocking

  • Variety: zone, gap, man, and combo blocks.
  • Get good angles and leverage.
  • Explode off the line, sustain, and finish.
  • Seal the edge and close the hole.
  • Drive defenders off the ball.
  • Finish every play.

2. Pass Protection

  • Proper set: feet, hands, and pad level.
  • Punch with your hands.
  • Anchor against the bull rush.
  • Mirror and stay in front.
  • Show awareness of stunts and blitzes.
  • Chip, then release into your route.

3. Receiving

  • Run routes at full speed with precision.
  • Get open against man and zone.
  • Show strong hands.
  • High-point the ball.
  • Break tackles and gain extra yards after the catch.
  • Show body control.

4. Red Zone / Goal Line

  • Be a big target who catches in traffic.
  • Use your size and body to shield defenders.
  • High-point touchdown catches.
  • Block and seal for the extra yards.
  • Win 50/50 balls.

5. Competitiveness & Effort

  • Finish every rep.
  • Hustle to the ball.
  • Celebrate teammates.
  • Bring sideline energy and leadership.

H · H-Back

The versatile moving piece. Alignments: off tackle (left or right), offset in the backfield, and wide or slot off the line.

Key roles: lead blocker, short-yardage and fullback role, play-action flat or swing routes, and occasional downfield receiver.

1. Lead Blocking / Run Game

  • Lead the way through the hole.
  • Get out in space and deliver a blow.
  • Maintain leverage while moving.
  • Make the linebacker miss.
  • Show cut blocks on linebackers and DBs.
  • Finish the block by driving your feet.
  • Open running lanes for big plays.

2. Versatility in Alignment

  • Line up in multiple spots: H, wing, and offset.
  • Show motion in, out, and across the formation.
  • Shift defenders with motion.
  • Create mismatches.

3. Receiving / Pass Game

  • Run every route with quick, reliable hands.
  • Run option routes against zone.
  • Work the middle of the field with seams and digs.
  • Catch and run in the screen game.
  • On play-action, sell the block, release, and catch.
  • Show downfield ability when asked.

4. Short Yardage / Goal Line

  • Run like a bowling ball and move the pile.
  • Push defenders and gain tough yards.
  • Find the end zone.
  • Be physical and dependable.

5. Football IQ & Execution

  • Know your assignments.
  • Make correct reads.
  • Adjust to defensive looks.
  • Be a problem-solver on the field.

F · Flex / Slot Tight End

The matchup problem. Alignments: flexed off the line, in the slot inside the numbers, and wide outside the numbers.

Key roles: slot receiver, mismatch against linebackers and DBs, move tight end, option-route runner, and RPO or quick-game target.

1. Route Running

  • Run crisp, precise routes.
  • Show the route tree: slant, speed out, stick, dig, corner, post, cross, whip, mesh, choice, and seam.
  • Create separation against DBs and linebackers.
  • Use releases: jab, swipe, and speed.
  • Win at the top of routes.

2. Getting Open vs. Coverage

  • Beat press coverage.
  • Use your body and hands.
  • Work the middle against zone.
  • Find soft spots.
  • Adjust routes based on coverage.

3. YAC / After the Catch

  • Catch and go.
  • Break tackles.
  • Make defenders miss.
  • Turn short catches into big plays.

4. RPO / Quick Game

  • Be a reliable quick target.
  • Read the defense and adjust.
  • Get open fast.
  • Make plays in space.

5. Versatility & Motion

  • Move all over the formation.
  • Show snaps in the slot, wide, at wing, and in the backfield.
  • Motion across the formation.
  • Line up next to receivers and create mismatches.

Present a 6–10 Minute Film

  • Quality over quantity.
  • Give every clip a purpose.
  • Start strong and end strong.
  • Use labels, arrows, and slow motion.

Extra Things to Include

  • Big plays: touchdowns and explosive plays.
  • Key third downs: conversions.
  • Two-minute offense: poise and execution.
  • Top competition: games against good teams.
  • Different looks: multiple formations and motions.
  • Special teams: punt and field-goal blocking, if applicable.

How to Make Your Film Stand Out

  • Start with your best plays; the first 30 seconds matter.
  • Use text or graphics for down, distance, score, and key information.
  • Cut dead time: no huddles and few pre-snap clips.
  • Show full speed, then slow motion for key moments.
  • Keep clips between 5 and 15 seconds.
  • End with a highlight-reel finish.

What College Coaches Are Looking For

  • Can you help us win on first, second, and third down?
  • Can you block and be physical in the run game?
  • Can you protect our quarterback?
  • Can you catch and be a threat in the passing game?
  • Can you create mismatches?
  • Are you tough, smart, and coachable?
  • Do you take pride in the details?
🏈
Final Tip

Be the player they can't take off the field. Block. Catch. Compete. Win.

01Can you get open?
02Can you make plays?

Highlights by Wide Receiver Role

X Receiver · Weak Side

Alignment: typically away from the tight end, often isolated on the boundary (short side of the field) in a one-on-one matchup with a cornerback.

Primary role: the outside go-to threat who wins one-on-one matchups and makes explosive plays downfield.

Best for Showing

01Speed
02Deep-threat ability
03One-on-one wins
04Contested catches

Routes & Situations to Show

  • Go routes
  • Posts
  • Deep corners
  • Comebacks
  • Outs
  • Digs
  • Back-shoulder fades
  • Red-zone and 50/50 balls
  • Third down and long yardage
  • Screens
  • End-arounds and reverses
  • Downfield blocking

Show Versatility

  • Show motions and slot reps.
  • Include different alignments.

Z Receiver · Strong Side / Tight End Side

Alignment: about one yard behind the line of scrimmage, usually on the same side as the tight end or another receiver. The off-the-line alignment helps avoid press coverage.

Motion and versatility: can motion before the snap. As a flanker, the Z receiver uses intermediate routes, jet sweeps, and movement inside or outside to create space.

Best for Showing

01Quickness
02Route running
03YAC ability
04Versatility

Routes & Situations to Show

  • Hitches
  • Outs
  • Slants
  • Shallow crosses
  • Choice routes
  • Sticks
  • Drags
  • Angles
  • Jet sweeps and orbit motion
  • Bubble and swing screens
  • Play-action and RPO
  • Third down and medium yardage

Show Versatility

  • Run plays from different alignments.
  • Move around the formation.

Slot Receiver · In the Slot

Flexibility: the slot is the primary spot, but versatile receivers also move around, including lining up outside.

Role: work the middle of the field and serve as a target on crucial short-yardage passing downs.

Matchup weapon: create mismatches against linebackers and smaller defenders.

Best for Showing

01Separation
02Hands
03Short-yardage production
04Football IQ

Routes & Situations to Show

  • Slants
  • Drags
  • Crossers
  • Option routes
  • Choice routes
  • Texas and levels concepts
  • Mesh
  • Shallow crosses
  • Bubble and tunnel screens
  • Third down and short yardage
  • Red-zone seams and quick outs
  • Motions, bunches, and stacks

Show Versatility

  • Show outside alignments and backfield reps.
  • Include motions.

Route Running

  • Crisp releases with no wasted steps.
  • Proper stems and speed into and out of breaks.
  • Sharp breaks and footwork.
  • Sell fakes and changes of direction.
  • Create separation at all levels.
  • Run the entire route through the whistle.

Hands & Catching

  • Extend and catch away from your body.
  • High-point the football.
  • Track the ball over your shoulder.
  • One-handed catches.
  • Contested-catch ability.
  • Body control and concentration.

Playmaking

  • Yards after the catch.
  • Be elusive in space.
  • Make defenders miss.
  • Turn short plays into big plays.
  • Show awareness of leverage.
  • Score when you get the ball.

Blocking & Effort

  • Be a willing blocker on every play.
  • Use proper technique.
  • Show downfield blocking.
  • Finish every rep with effort.
  • Hustle on every play.
  • Bring great teammate energy.

Football IQ

  • Understand assignments.
  • Read coverages.
  • Adjust to leverage.
  • Find soft spots in zone.
  • Make smart decisions.
  • Know down and distance.

Physical Tools

  • Top-end speed.
  • Burst and acceleration.
  • Change of direction.
  • Jump-ball ability.
  • Body control.
  • Length and play strength.

Build Your Film the Right Way

  • Quality over quantity: 10–15 minutes of your best plays beats 30-plus minutes of everything.
  • Front-load your film: put your best plays at the beginning and hook coaches in the first 30 seconds.
  • Show variety: include routes, situations, alignments, YAC, blocking, and all aspects of your game.
  • Know your role: tailor the film to X, Z, or Slot, while showing versatility.
  • Include information: add your name, height, weight, GPA, class year, and contact information.

Technical Requirements

  • Film in widescreen; avoid vertical phone clips.
  • Use All-22 film when possible for the best evaluation.
  • Use end-zone and sideline angles.
  • Include game sound so coaches can listen to effort and calls.
  • Edit out dead time, including huddles and time between plays.
  • Use high-quality, well-lit footage.

Situations to Include

  • First down: show consistency.
  • Second and medium: sustained drives at 3–7 yards.
  • Third down: show short-, medium-, and long-yardage conversions.
  • Red zone: execute under pressure.

Don't Forget to Show

  • Pre-snap alignments: wide, slot, and motion.
  • How you move and get lined up in motion.
  • Routes against man coverage.
  • Routes against zone coverage.
  • Adjustments to coverage.
  • Chemistry with your quarterback: trust and connection.
  • Celebrations that show personality and production.
  • Effort on every play; coaches notice.

What to Avoid

  • Too much filler: long games, huddles, or breaks in the action.
  • Vertical phone video.
  • Bad angles: behind the play or too far away.
  • Only highlights; show full routes sometimes.
  • Penalties, drops, and mistakes, unless showing how you bounce back.
  • Over-editing with music and graphics.

Bonus Tips

  • Send your film to coaches early and follow up.
  • Keep your film updated weekly.
  • Personalize emails and build relationships.
  • Be active on social media, including X/Twitter and Hudl.
  • Highlight academics, attitude, and your GPA.
🏈
Final Tip

Your film is your resume. Make every rep count. Earn their attention. Earn your opportunity.

Film highlights: what to show college coaches

QB

Show that you are a smart, accurate, and confident quarterback who can make all the throws, lead your team, and win in big moments.

Quarterback Highlight Film Blueprint

Overall Goal

Show coaches you can make every throw, lead your team, and win when the moment is biggest.

Position-Specific Highlights

01Accuracy & Ball Placement: Short, intermediate, and deep accuracy; throw receivers open; show touch on deep balls, tight-window placement, consistent footwork, and every throw in the playbook.
02Decision Making: Read defenses before and after the snap, identify coverages and rotations, make the right read, take what the defense gives, avoid turnovers, and check down when needed.
03Pocket Presence: Stay calm under pressure, navigate the pocket, move with purpose, escape and extend plays, keep your eyes downfield, and avoid negative plays and sacks.
04Arm Talent & Velocity: Show arm strength on all routes, make every throw, throw on the run, drive deep balls, layer throws, and throw with confidence.
05Leadership & Command: Show huddle leadership, command at the line, rally the team, poise in big moments, game-winning drives, and competitive toughness.
06Mobility & Athleticism: Use your legs when it is there, scramble effectively, buy time, make plays outside the pocket, keep drives alive, and be a threat with your legs.

Physical Traits & Prototype

  • Height and size: ideal QB range shown as 6'1"–6'5", 180–230+ lbs.
  • Arm strength: deep-ball velocity and zip.
  • Athleticism: speed, agility, mobility, and balance.
  • Durability: ability to take hits and stay healthy.

Technical Skills

  • Footwork: base, timing, drops, and alignment.
  • Mechanics: throwing motion, release, and consistency.
  • Hand placement: proper grip, control, and follow-through.
  • Route discipline: execute RPOs, play action, and timing.

Mentality & IQ

  • Football IQ: understand coverages, fronts, and blitzes.
  • Preparation: show study habits and command of the playbook.
  • Process: handle adversity, mistakes, and pressure.
  • Competitor: want the ball in big moments.

Intangibles

  • Leadership: command respect in the locker room.
  • Communication: be clear and consistent.
  • Work ethic: film, weight room, and practice habits.
  • Character: coachability, accountability, and maturity.

What to Include in Your Film

01Full-game film: show consistency from start to finish.
02Best games: include quality opponents.
03Show all throws: short, mid-range, deep, and on the run.
04Big situations: red zone, third down, two-minute, and clutch moments.
05Smart decisions: limit mistakes and turnovers and show how you handle tough plays.
🏈
Final Tip

Your film is your resume. Make every rep count. Earn their attention. Earn your opportunity.

High school cornerbacks: what to highlight on film

CB

Show college coaches you can lock down and make plays. Win your matchup consistently, make plays on the ball, and help your team win.

Cornerback Highlight Film Blueprint

What College Coaches Want to See

01Football IQ: Diagnose, react, and make the right play.
02Speed & Agility: Run, change direction, and stay with anyone.
03Man Coverage: Lock up receivers one-on-one.
04Ball Skills: Make plays on the ball.
05Consistency: Repeat your performance against good competition.
06Physicality: Tackle well and compete every snap.

1. Pre-Snap & Recognition

  • Show alignment variety: press, off, inside, and outside.
  • Identify route combinations.
  • Communicate coverage and leverage.
  • Check the QB and understand formation and motion.

2. Press & Jam Technique

  • Show explosive press technique.
  • Keep hands inside and deliver a good punch.
  • Control the receiver at the line.
  • Disrupt timing without drawing flags.

3. Man Coverage Skills

  • Mirror and stay in phase.
  • Turn your hips and run.
  • Show eye discipline; don't peek.
  • Use your hands without holding.
  • Stay with the receiver through the catch.

4. Zone Coverage & Awareness

  • Show drops, depth, and landmarks.
  • Read the QB and route combinations.
  • Key and break on the ball.
  • Pass off routes.
  • Communicate switches and matchups.

5. Ball Skills & Turnovers

  • Interceptions and pass breakups.
  • Swat and bat balls down.
  • High-point the ball.
  • Play through the ball.
  • Show body control in the air.

6. Run Support & Tackling

  • Tackle in space.
  • Take proper pursuit angles.
  • Force plays inside.
  • Fill the alley.
  • Finish every tackle.
  • Show reliable tackling without misses.

7. Compete & Finish

  • Give effort every snap.
  • Finish the play.
  • Compete with confidence and sportsmanship.
  • Celebrate plays.
  • Lead by example.

Types of Plays to Include

01Man coverage: press man, off man, trail technique, jump balls, and back-shoulder coverage.
02Zone coverage: Cover 2 and 3, quarters and Cover 4, robber, pattern match, and palms or cloud.
03Transitions: press to off, off to bail, zone to man, man to zone, and switches.
04Playmaking: interceptions, pass breakups, forced fumbles, touchdown-saving plays, and fourth-down stops.
05Big-game clips: top receivers, strong teams, critical situations, and game-winning plays.

Present a 6–10 Minute Film

  • Quality over quantity.
  • Give every clip a purpose.
  • Start strong and end strong.
  • Use labels, arrows, and slow motion.

What to Show Throughout Your Film

  • Start with explosive plays.
  • Include varied matchups, coverages, and formations.
  • Show plays from multiple angles: sideline and end zone.
  • Use slow motion to show technique and details.
  • Keep clips short, 3–8 seconds, and to the point.

Cornerback Measurables

  • Speed: 40-yard dash and 10/20 split.
  • Change of direction: shuttle, 3-cone, and pro agility.
  • Explosion: vertical jump and broad jump.
  • Physical: strength, tackling, and physical play.

Extra Tips to Stand Out

  • Show versatility: outside, slot, and nickel.
  • Show leadership in the huddle and through communication.
  • Highlight academics and character moments, if possible.
  • Keep your best plays early.
  • End with a highlight-reel finish.

Extra Things to Include

  • Big plays: interceptions, breakups, and fumble recoveries.
  • Key third downs: critical stops.
  • Red zone and goal line: lock-down plays.
  • Two-minute defense: clutch stops.
  • Top receivers: show your best matchups.
  • Special teams: punt and kick coverage, plus returns.

What College Coaches Are Looking For

  • Can you shut down their best receiver?
  • Can you play multiple coverages?
  • Do you have great ball skills?
  • Can you tackle in space?
  • Do you compete every snap?
  • Do you communicate and lead?
  • Do you make game-changing plays?
  • Do you take pride in your details?
🏈
Final Tip

Lock down. Make plays. Change the game. That's what cornerbacks do.

High school linebackers: what to highlight on film

LB

Off-ball linebacker film should show that you diagnose, attack, and finish. Show coaches your instincts, range, production, consistency, and physicality.

Linebacker Highlight Film Blueprint

What College Coaches Want to See

01Football IQ: Diagnose, react, and make the right play.
02Athleticism: Run, change direction, cover, and close.
03Production: Make plays that impact the game.
04Consistency: Repeat your performance against good competition.
05Physicality: Shed blocks, tackle well, and finish.

1. Pre-Snap: Instincts & Football IQ

  • Show alignment variety: stand-up, stacked, and wide 9.
  • Point out formation, strength, and motion adjustments.
  • Communicate green-dot calls, checks, and alerts.
  • Show checks to your position teammates.
  • Identify keys: run/pass, QB intent, and backfield action.
  • Show patience versus play action.

2. Diagnosis & Reads

  • Read and react to run versus pass.
  • Show you can key and diagnose quickly.
  • React to play-side versus away-from-play action.
  • Trigger downhill on run plays.
  • Drop with your eyes on the QB in pass plays.
  • Recognize screens, draws, and misdirection.

3. Athleticism (Must Show)

  • Show sideline-to-sideline speed and closing speed to the ball.
  • Change direction and accelerate.
  • Backpedal, shuffle, and transition.
  • Show range in coverage.
  • Jump to deflect passes and compete for contested plays.
  • Burst through the line into gaps.
  • Recover quickly when out of position.

4. Coverage Skills

  • Show coverage drops: hook, curl, flat, and seam.
  • Match up with RBs, TEs, and slot receivers.
  • Show zone coverage awareness and positioning.
  • Read QB eyes and anticipate throws.
  • Break on the ball and make a play on it.
  • Defend passes and show versatility in multiple coverages.

5. Run Defense

  • Come downhill and fill with power.
  • Take on blocks and shed effectively.
  • Use proper leverage and angles.
  • Tackle in space and stop the run behind the line of scrimmage.
  • Pursue plays from the backside.
  • Show gang tackling and team run fits.

6. Blitzing Ability

  • Show timed blitzes and vary the looks: A-gap, B-gap, edge, and delayed.
  • Explode off the line.
  • Win with speed, power, or technique.
  • Finish the play: sacks, tackles for loss, and hurries.

7. Physicality & Finish

  • Deliver a pop when hitting.
  • Wrap up and drive through tackles.
  • Finish every play.
  • Show aggression and play with violence.
  • Include big-hit clips and celebrate plays with energy.

8. Big-Game / Big-Play Highlights

  • Show plays against good offenses and athletes.
  • Highlight turnovers forced: interceptions, forced fumbles, and recoveries.
  • Include game-changing plays: fourth-down stops and goal-line stands.
  • Show clutch moments in close games.
  • Highlight leadership in key situations.

Athleticism Showcase: Must-Have Clips

  • Chase plays from sideline to sideline.
  • Backpedal and turn to run with a receiver.
  • High-point jumps and tipped passes.
  • Accelerate to close on a screen.
  • Change direction to avoid blocks.
  • Show long-pursuit and recovery plays.
  • Show blitz pickup and speed-rush wins.
  • Pair film with measurables: 40-yard dash, shuttle, and broad jump.

Recommended Film Length & Structure

  • Total length: 3–5 minutes.
  • Clip length: 3–8 seconds.
  • Total clips: 40–60 plays.
  • Suggested order: intro, run defense, man/zone coverage, blitzes, big plays and turnovers, then game highlights and leadership.

Do

  • Edit the film to your best plays.
  • Keep clips short and to the point; start with your best plays.
  • Include stats and game information.
  • Show full speed and multiple angles when possible.
  • Use titles or text to explain plays.
  • Organize your film by category.

Don't

  • Include penalties or mistakes.
  • Make the film too long.
  • Include plays where you are not involved.
  • Use music or extra effects.
  • Forget athletic plays in coverage.

Extra Things to Include

  • Big plays: takeaways and explosive plays.
  • Key third downs: conversions.
  • Two-minute offense: poise and execution.
  • Top competition: games against good teams.
  • Different looks: multiple formations and motions.
  • Special teams: punt and field-goal blocking, if applicable.

Extra Tips to Stand Out

  • Show versatility across positions and packages.
  • Display leadership on and off the field.
  • Update your film regularly.
  • Include your recruiting profile: Hudl, NCSA, RecruitMe, etc.
🏈
Final Tip

Be the guy they can't take off the field. Diagnose. Attack. Finish. Dominate.

College Coaches Are Watching for Three Things

When to Contact Coaches

  • Send updates after meaningful events: strong game film, improved test numbers, or GPA wins.
  • Reach out ahead of key windows: camps, visits, and dead/evaluation periods.
  • Avoid random messages with no new value.
  • Be consistent enough to stay visible but not so frequent that messages feel repetitive.

Email Template Structure

  • Subject line: name, class year, position, school.
  • Opening: short intro and one-line reason for contacting that specific program.
  • Middle: one update plus film link and core measurables.
  • Close: one clear ask, such as camp attendance or feedback request.
  • Signature: full name, phone, email, high school, coach contact, and profile link.

Texting Etiquette

  • Keep messages brief and easy to answer.
  • Use proper grammar and punctuation; avoid slang-heavy messages.
  • Do not text repeatedly if there is no reply yet.
  • Text at reasonable times in the coach's time zone.

Calling Coaches

  • Prepare three talking points before you call: update, fit question, and next step.
  • Practice a 20-second introduction so you sound confident and clear.
  • If you get voicemail, leave a concise message and follow up once by text or email.
  • After calls, log notes and action items so you can follow up accurately.

Social Media Best Practices

  • Pin key recruiting posts: latest film, measurables, and contact info.
  • Keep posts aligned with your brand as a disciplined student-athlete.
  • Never post content that could raise maturity or character concerns.
  • Engage respectfully when interacting with programs and staff.

Following Up Without Overdoing It

  • If no response, wait and follow up with a real update, not just checking in.
  • Space messages based on recruiting calendar and relationship strength.
  • Use one communication lane at a time before switching channels.
  • Always keep your tone professional, patient, and respectful.

Which Camps Matter?

  • Prioritize camps where schools you already communicate with will actually evaluate your position.
  • Focus on quality over quantity: a few strong performances beat a packed but unfocused schedule.
  • Use camp choices to answer your current recruiting question: exposure, validation, or offer movement.
  • Avoid events that do not provide position-specific testing or coach interaction.

Prospect Camps vs. Showcases

  • Prospect camps usually involve direct coaching by college staffs and can impact board placement quickly.
  • Showcases can provide broad exposure and testing, but direct school-specific feedback may be limited.
  • If your goal is an offer from School X, their camp often matters more than a general event.
  • Use showcases to create initial awareness, then follow up with targeted school camps.

How to Prepare

  • Train for the exact drills you will perform: starts, transitions, and position movement.
  • Plan hydration, sleep, and nutrition 48 hours before camp day.
  • Arrive with a short self-introduction and your recruiting profile links ready.
  • Set one performance goal and one relationship goal before each event.

What to Bring

  • Cleats, backup cleats, gloves, and weather-appropriate gear.
  • Water, electrolyte options, and simple fuel snacks.
  • Notebook or notes app for coach feedback and next steps.
  • Printable profile sheet or digital QR link for easy follow-up.

Following Up Afterward

  • Send a short thank-you message within 24 hours.
  • Reference one specific drill or coaching point to show attention to detail.
  • Share updated film when you apply their feedback in games or workouts.
  • Ask one clear next-step question: visit, call, or camp return date.

When a Camp Is Worth the Investment

  • It has direct access to coaches on your target list.
  • You are healthy and prepared to perform near your best.
  • The event timing supports your recruiting window, not just your schedule.
  • You can clearly explain what outcome you expect from attending.

Official vs. Unofficial Visits

  • Official visits are school-funded and usually happen later in recruiting. Use these for final fit decisions.
  • Unofficial visits are self-funded and can happen earlier. Use them to gather early information and build relationships.
  • On any visit, ask for your realistic role and timeline, not just what sounds good in the moment.
  • If possible, visit during both a game weekend and a normal school week so you see both versions of campus life.

Questions to Ask Coaches

  • Where exactly do you project me in your scheme and depth chart over the next 2 years?
  • What specific skills or metrics do I need to improve to earn early playing time?
  • How do you develop players at my position week to week?
  • How stable is the staff, and who will coach my room day to day?
  • What does off-season training and recovery look like for my position group?

What Coaches Are Evaluating

  • How you interact with everyone: staff, players, hosts, and support workers.
  • Body language during meetings, campus tours, and downtime.
  • How well your personality fits the locker room and culture.
  • Whether your family communication style matches the program environment.
  • Your maturity: punctuality, respect, and ability to ask thoughtful questions.

Campus Visit Checklist

  • Tour football spaces: locker room, training room, weight room, meeting rooms, and recovery options.
  • Tour academics: tutoring center, major department, class buildings, and advisor support.
  • Meet current players in your position room and ask what daily life is really like.
  • Review distance from home, travel logistics, weather, and housing.
  • Write notes right after the visit before details fade.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Different staff members give conflicting messages about your role or scholarship status.
  • Current players quietly warn you about communication or trust issues.
  • No clear development plan at your position.
  • Pressure to commit immediately before you finish comparisons.
  • Promises are made verbally but never documented or explained in detail.

Academic Fit

  • Major/field fit: what school or field are you most interested in?
  • Admission path: do you need separate application/acceptance into that college (for example, business or communications)?
  • Weekly schedule reality: how many classes per day and what does a typical week look like in-season and off-season?
  • Class/credit structure: are there limits on classes you can take, and are options online, in-person, or both?
  • Graduation roadmap: how many credits are required, and are credits transferable if your path changes?
  • Help/tutors: what tutoring access is available, where is it located, and what does the study hall/program actually include?
  • Career development: what formal career development programming exists for athletes?
  • Academic support and graduation outcomes: ask about graduation rates and post-college placement support.

Facilities

  • Do the day-to-day facilities help you become the best player on and off the field?
  • Can you train and develop effectively and efficiently in those spaces?
  • Do you personally like the environment and feel comfortable there?
  • Locker room: you will spend major time here, so make sure it fits your needs and comfort.
  • Practice fields/indoor: can you master your craft day or night; evaluate surface quality, weather options, and training tools (bags, jugs, etc.).
  • Weight room: check rack availability and whether there is adequate indoor speed-training area.
  • Training/recovery room: does it include complete recovery resources (hydro, float tanks, cryo, nap/sleep area, sauna/steam, etc.)?
  • Position meeting rooms: are they spacious, updated, and useful for your specific position group?
  • Player lounge: another space you will use often, so it should support comfort and reset time.
  • Nutrition hubs/stations: one or multiple, self-serve or staffed, and do they offer what you need?
  • Study center: is it convenient and easy to use consistently?
  • Parking: understand athlete parking costs, availability, and fine/ticket policies.

Coaching

  • Coaching/support staff: are they genuine, and do you and your family feel comfortable around them?
  • Head coach: is he established at the university, and what is his stability (contract years / hot-seat risk)?
  • Coordinator/position coach: does the scheme maximize your chance of success?
  • Ask for details on their tendencies: run/pass ratio, man/zone ratio, and blitz usage.
  • Ask where they project you positionally (for example: WR X/W/Z, DT nose/1-tech/3-tech).
  • Strength and conditioning: 1-on-1 vs group work, staff-to-player ratio, and summer/spring plan details.
  • Ask if they are using modern sports-science methods and recovery/training best practices.

Exposure

  • What is the program's short- and long-term plan for player promotion?
  • Are there new promotion opportunities or activities in motion?
  • Do they have a specific exposure plan for you, with examples from current top players?
  • What does their branding program include for athletes?
  • What media expectations exist, and what opportunities are available to build your brand?
  • Do they provide social-media education/development programming (group sessions or individual)?
  • Ask for direct feedback on your current brand: what is strong, what is weak, and what to improve now and later.

Support Staff

  • You may spend as much (or more) time with support staff as with coaches, so evaluate this group carefully.
  • Equipment staff: attitude and daily interactions matter; respectful relationships here pay off.
  • Nutrition staff: ask about nutritionists/chefs/serving support and whether communication is athlete-centered.
  • Strength assistants: who works with your position group, and what extra development/recovery help do they provide?
  • Medical team: understand trainer access and doctor network coverage for injuries and long-term care.
  • Operations/player development/front office/security: ask how they support athletes and families during problems.

Traditions

  • What traditions define the program (gameday, kickoff windows, bands/tailgates/rivalry atmosphere)?
  • What is unique about this program compared to peer schools?
  • Where does the program stand within its conference today?
  • Which program tradition impressed you most, and why?
  • Can you and your family genuinely see yourselves enjoying that gameday environment?

Football Opportunity

  • Depth chart and realistic path to playing time.
  • Scheme fit for your position and skillset.
  • Coaching staff stability and development history.

Environment & Lifestyle

  • Distance from home and support network.
  • Campus culture and athlete expectations.
  • City, weather, and day-to-day comfort.

Financial & Future

  • Scholarship details and out-of-pocket cost.
  • NIL opportunity and support structure.
  • Network value beyond football.
